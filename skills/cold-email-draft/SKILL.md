# Cold Email Draft

Use this skill when the user wants a cold email, a follow-up, or a short sequence. **Draft only. Never send, schedule, or auto-mail.** Even if a tool is connected, stop after the draft unless the user clearly names the recipient and says to send.

## Steps

1. Confirm the sender's identity, the offer, and why this recipient (or segment) might care. If the list is vague, write a template with `[Name]`, `[company]`, and `[trigger]` placeholders.
2. Subject lines: 2–3 options, short, specific, no clickbait, no ALL CAPS, no fake "Re:".
3. Body: 5–8 sentences max for a first touch. First line is about them or a verifiable trigger, not your biography. One ask (a reply, a 15-minute call, a link to read).
4. Write like a person. Cut `I hope this email finds you well`, `synergy`, and fake personalization ("I was so impressed by your recent activities").
5. Optional: a shorter follow-up that adds one new fact, not "just bumping this."
6. Spell out what the user must personalize before anyone hits send.

## Rules

- **Do not send.** Do not enroll anyone in a sequence. Do not scrape or guess private emails you were not given.
- Do not invent case studies, mutual friends, or "loved your post" if the user did not provide the post.
- No deceptive headers, spoofing, or harvested-list language. Mention that the user should honor applicable email and spam rules (and unsubscribe if this becomes a campaign).
- If the user later asks to send, require an explicit recipient and the final text, and still prefer they send it themselves unless they have already authorized a specific send.

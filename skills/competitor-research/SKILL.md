# Competitor Research

Use this skill when the user wants a comparison of competitors or an alternative to their product. Stick to information you can observe or that they provided. Do not pretend you have private financials.

## Steps

1. Confirm the user's product, the market slice, and the competitor names or URLs. If they ask "who competes with X," list candidates as hypotheses and say so.
2. For each competitor, capture (when available): who it is for, core offer, notable features, pricing model (and the date you saw it), positioning language, and distribution (docs, community, ads, sales-led).
3. Build a comparison table on a small number of dimensions that matter to the user's decision (e.g. target customer, pricing shape, standout feature, obvious gap).
4. Note gaps and risks for the user — "they are weaker at X" only with evidence (missing from pricing page, docs, or trial). Label inference as inference.
5. End with 3–5 implications: where the user might differentiate, what to verify by actually using the product, and what you could not find.

## Rules

- Do not scrape behind logins or paywalls you do not have access to. Do not invent prices.
- Do not recommend illegal or unethical competitive tactics (scraping private data, impersonation).
- Dates matter. Write "as of <date>" next to pricing and plan names.
- This is a research brief, not legal or investment advice.
